At the very tip of the Machars peninsula — that long finger of land south of Wigtown reaching into the Solway Firth — the Isle of Whithorn sits at the end of the road, quite literally. It is a tiny fishing village with a working harbour, the ruins of St Ninian's Chapel, and an inn that has been serving travellers and sailors for generations. Getting here requires a deliberate decision: this is not somewhere you pass through. It is somewhere you go to, and the Steam Packet Inn is precisely why people do.

## The Setting

The Steam Packet sits right on the harbourfront, looking out over the boats and the open Solway beyond. The village is one of the most evocative in southwest Scotland — fishing creels on the pier, seabirds overhead, and on a clear day a view across to the Cumbrian hills. The pub has earned its reputation over decades by being exactly what a remote harbourside inn should be: warm, unpretentious, and worth the drive.

## The Bar and Food

Inside, the atmosphere is what you would hope for: proper ales, a good selection of Scottish spirits, and a kitchen that makes full use of the harbour on its doorstep. Solway scallops are a particular speciality here — landed locally and cooked simply, they are among the best reasons to make the trip. The seafood menu changes with the catch, and for those who prefer something from the land, Galloway beef and game are handled with equal care. This is not fancy food, but it is genuinely good food, cooked with access to exceptional ingredients.

## Dog-Friendly Details

Dogs are welcome both in the bar and at the outdoor tables on the harbourfront — a combination that works well for a dog arriving from a long coastal walk. Water bowls appear without being asked. The pub has the relaxed attitude to four-legged guests that comes naturally to somewhere used to welcoming walkers and sailors, both of whom tend to arrive with muddy or salty companions.

## Walking Nearby

The Steam Packet sits at the start of one of the finest remote coastal walks in Dumfries and Galloway: the route to Burrow Head and St Ninian's Cave, a five-mile circular across dramatic sea cliffs. St Ninian's Cave is said to be where Scotland's first Christian missionary retreated for private prayer — one of the earliest Christian sites in Scotland, reached via a pebble beach and a short cliff path. The walk is entirely suitable for dogs on leads along the cliff sections, and the return to the harbour village with a meal and a drink at the Steam Packet makes it one of the most complete dog-friendly day-out combinations in the region.

## Practical Information

Isle of Whithorn is approximately 15 miles south of Newton Stewart via the A714 and B7004. Parking is available on the harbourfront. Opening hours can vary seasonally — it is worth calling ahead or checking locally before making the journey in winter or early spring.
