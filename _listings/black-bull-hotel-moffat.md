Moffat's most atmospheric pub occupies a 16th-century coaching inn on the High Street, and it is exactly the kind of place that rewards arriving muddy and cold after a day on the hills. Low-beamed ceilings, stone floors, and open fires that earn their keep through a long Borders winter — the Black Bull Hotel has been doing this for four centuries and it shows.

## The Bar

The bar retains the character that a coaching inn of this age ought to have. The heavy beams, stone floors, and scale of the fireplaces feel authentic rather than stage-set — the accumulated gravity of a pub that has served travellers since the 16th century. It is an excellent place to settle in after covering ground on the Moffat Hills or the Southern Upland Way, and that combination of atmosphere and welcome puts it among the better post-walk pubs in this corner of Scotland.

The ales are well-kept and the whisky selection reflects the serious drinking culture of the Scottish Borders. Food is honest and substantial: the kind of cooking you want after hill country — good soups, local meat, warming mains.

## Dogs at the Black Bull

Dogs are welcome in the bar area throughout. The stone-flagged section near the entrance handles muddy arrivals without fuss, which is practically useful in a town that sits at the gateway to some of the most demanding dog-walking country in Dumfries and Galloway. Water is available on request.

## Walking from Moffat

Grey Mare's Tail, around 10 miles northeast of Moffat on the A708 toward Selkirk, is the most dramatic local walk: a 60-metre waterfall managed by the National Trust for Scotland with a steep, well-maintained path and extraordinary views over Loch Skeen above. Dogs must be kept on leads on the NTS path. For something closer to town, the Moffat Hills offer open moorland walking directly from the edge of town, with the route toward Gallow Hill giving sweeping views down Annandale.

## Getting There

Moffat sits just off the A74(M) at junction 15 — a natural stop on a longer journey north or south. The Black Bull is on the High Street, clearly visible from the main town square. Parking is available on the High Street and in the car park behind the square.
