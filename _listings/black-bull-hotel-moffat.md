Moffat is one of the most visited towns in Dumfries and Galloway — a natural stopping point on the A74(M) corridor and a genuine destination in its own right, with the Moffat Hills and Grey Mare's Tail providing some of the most dramatic walking in the south of Scotland. After a day on those hills, or on arrival into town after a long drive north, The Black Bull Hotel is the obvious first port of call for dogs and their owners.

## History and Atmosphere

The Black Bull has been serving travellers on Moffat's High Street since the 16th century. It is the town's most atmospheric pub — the kind of place that takes time and genuine use to acquire the character it has. Low-beamed ceilings, open fires that earn their keep on cold Annandale days, and a layout that feels like it grew organically out of centuries of use rather than being designed to look old. It is, in the best sense, exactly what a Scottish coaching inn should be.

## Dogs at the Black Bull

The bar is dog-friendly throughout, and the staff have the relaxed attitude to four-legged guests that comes from long experience of welcoming walkers off the hills. Water bowls are available. Well-behaved dogs are welcome at the bar and at tables in the bar area — they will not be pointed at a draughty corner near the door.

## Food and Drink

Food is honest pub cooking that hits the spot after a long walk: generous portions, local produce where possible, and the kind of solid, warming menu that Galloway hill days demand. Real ales are well-kept; the whisky selection covers all the bases.

## Combining with a Walk

Moffat is ideally placed for combining a pub stop with serious walking. Grey Mare's Tail — one of Scotland's finest waterfalls — is around ten miles northeast on the A708. The Moffat Hills above the town offer moorland walking accessible directly from the High Street. Arriving at the Black Bull after either feels entirely earned.
