The Black Bull Hotel on Moffat's High Street is one of the most atmospheric places to eat and drink in Dumfries and Galloway — a coaching inn that has been welcoming travellers, walkers, and their dogs since the 16th century. The building wears its age well: low-beamed ceilings, stone floors worn smooth over centuries, and open fires that earn their place on a cold Galloway day. It is the kind of pub that makes you feel the effort of the drive was worthwhile.

## The Bar

The bar at the Black Bull is dog-friendly throughout — and genuinely so, not the grudging kind where your dog is permitted in one draughty corner near the door. This is a pub where walkers arrive straight from the Moffat Hills and the bar accommodates both them and their dogs without ceremony. Water bowls are available on request, the staff are relaxed and experienced with four-legged guests, and the stone-floored sections make muddy arrivals from the hills a non-issue.

The atmosphere is exactly right for a pub of this age and setting: unhurried, local, and comfortable without being self-conscious about it. Real ales are well-kept and the whisky selection reflects the Dumfries and Galloway tradition properly.

## Food

The food at the Black Bull leans on the excellent larder that Moffat's surrounding countryside provides. Galloway beef, hill lamb, local game in season, and straightforward cooking that does justice to the quality of the ingredients. This is hearty, satisfying pub food — exactly what you want after a morning on the hills or a long drive up the A74 corridor.

Portions are generous and the menu covers both lighter lunches and full evening meals. The Black Bull is particularly well-suited to a post-walk lunch: the kind of pub where you can take your time, let your dog dry off under the table, and order a second round without anyone looking impatient.

## Moffat as a Dog-Friendly Base

Moffat is one of the most rewarding dog-friendly destinations in Dumfries and Galloway, and the Black Bull sits at the natural end-point of several excellent walks. **Grey Mare's Tail**, around 10 miles northeast on the A708, is one of Scotland's most spectacular waterfalls and the path to it is dog-friendly on leads. The **Moffat Hills** are accessible directly from the town's edge, with open moorland walking on the Gallow Hill route offering sweeping views back down Annandale. The first miles of the **Annandale Way**, which begins in Moffat, follow the River Annan on easy, dog-friendly ground.

The town itself is compact, attractive, and well-set-up for visitors arriving with dogs. Keep dogs on leads on the High Street and in the town centre generally.

## Getting There and Parking

Moffat sits at the head of Annandale, around 55 miles north of Dumfries via the A701 and conveniently just off the A74(M) at junction 15. It is a natural stop for anyone driving into or out of Scotland via the west coast route.

Parking is available in the town centre car parks off the High Street and on some side streets. The Black Bull is in the heart of the town, easily walkable from any of the main car parks.

- **Opening hours:** Check directly with the pub — hours can vary seasonally.
- **Nearest walks:** Grey Mare's Tail (10 miles, A708), Moffat Hills (edge of town), Annandale Way start (town centre)
- **Getting there:** A74(M) junction 15, then A701 into town — under 5 minutes from the motorway
