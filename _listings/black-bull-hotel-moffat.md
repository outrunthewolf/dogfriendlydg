The Black Bull Hotel has stood on Moffat's High Street since the 16th century, and it shows — in the best possible way. Low-beamed ceilings, stone floors worn smooth over hundreds of years, and a wood-burning stove that earns its place on cold Galloway days. It is the kind of pub that resists improvement because it has already found its form.

For dog owners, the Black Bull is the natural end-point of a day in Moffat. Whether you have been up to Grey Mare's Tail, across the Moffat Hills, or simply walked the Annandale Way out of town, the bar welcomes muddy boots and wet dogs with the same equanimity. Water bowls are available, and the staff take the sight of four-legged guests in their stride.

## The Bar

The bar at the Black Bull is properly atmospheric — a place that repays lingering over a good pint. Real ales are well-kept and the whisky selection reflects the depth of the Scottish tradition. The food menu covers honest, filling pub cooking suited to walkers who have put in serious miles: hearty soups, local meat, warming mains. Nothing pretentious; everything reliable.

In winter, the open fire makes the bar one of the most comfortable rooms in the county. In summer, the bar retains the cool and calm of an old stone building — welcome on a warm August afternoon after a morning on the hillside.

## Moffat and the Surrounding Country

Moffat sits at the head of Annandale, surrounded by dramatic hill country that makes it one of the most rewarding bases in Dumfries and Galloway for active dog owners.

**Grey Mare's Tail** — around 10 miles northeast of Moffat on the A708 — is the centrepiece of any walking visit. This 60-metre waterfall managed by the National Trust for Scotland is one of the most spectacular in Scotland, and dogs are welcome on leads on the well-maintained path up to the falls and the remote Loch Skeen above. Allow two to three hours and bring plenty of water for your dog.

**The Moffat Hills** provide moorland walking directly from the edge of town. The route up towards Gallow Hill from the south end of Moffat is well-used by locals with dogs and gives wide views back down Annandale — a good leg-stretcher even on a short visit.

**The Annandale Way** begins in Moffat and the first few miles along the River Annan are gentle and enjoyable for most dogs — flat riverside walking through pleasant farmland, easily done as an out-and-back.

## Getting to Moffat

Moffat sits just off the A74(M) at junction 15, making it an exceptionally accessible stop on a longer journey between England and the Scottish Highlands. It is around 55 miles north of Dumfries on the A701 and just over an hour from Edinburgh or Glasgow. Parking is straightforward, with a town centre car park off Holm Street.

## Practical Information

- **Address:** High Street, Moffat, DG10 9EG
