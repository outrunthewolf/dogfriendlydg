Gatehouse of Fleet is one of the most satisfying small towns in southwest Scotland to visit with a dog. The Georgian high street is broad, handsome, and easy to walk; the countryside around it delivers beaches, a ruined castle, and forest hills within a few miles in every direction; and the concentration of dog-friendly pubs, cafes, and visitor attractions in and around the town is genuinely hard to match anywhere else in Dumfries and Galloway. It is the kind of place you arrive at for a morning and leave, several hours later, wondering why you do not come more often.

---

## Dog-Friendly Pubs and Dining in Gatehouse of Fleet

Gatehouse has more dog-friendly options for eating and drinking than most towns of its size, and all three main venues genuinely welcome dogs rather than merely permitting them.

### The Murray Arms Hotel

[The Murray Arms Hotel](/listings/the-murray-arms-gatehouse/) on the High Street is a coaching inn with real history — Robert Burns is said to have written 'Scots Wha Hae' here in 1793. Today it is one of the best dog-friendly pubs in Dumfries and Galloway. Dogs are welcome in the bar, water bowls and treats are on hand, and the food draws on excellent Galloway produce: local beef and lamb, Solway seafood, and seasonal dairy. The inn also takes dogs in bedrooms if you are staying overnight.

### The Masonic Arms

[The Masonic Arms](/listings/the-masonic-arms-gatehouse/) on Ann Street — a short walk from the Murray Arms — offers a more intimate atmosphere: low ceilings, stone walls, a wood-burning stove, and an excellent selection of real ales. Dogs are thoroughly at home here. It is the kind of village local that does not try to be anything it is not, and is all the better for it.

### Cream o' Galloway

[Cream o' Galloway](/listings/cream-o-galloway/) at Rainton Farm, a couple of miles outside the town, is one of Galloway's most-loved visitor attractions — an organic dairy farm producing award-winning ice cream. Dogs are welcome throughout the outdoor areas and on the farm nature trail. The outdoor seating area is available in most weathers, and the ice cream alone is worth the detour.

For a broader survey of where to eat across the region, see the [dog-friendly dining guide for Dumfries and Galloway](/guides/food-and-drink/dog-friendly-dining-dumfries-galloway/) and the [best dog-friendly bars in Dumfries and Galloway](/guides/food-and-drink/dog-friendly-bars-dumfries-galloway/).

## Dog-Friendly Walks and Beaches Near Gatehouse

### Cardoness Castle

[Cardoness Castle](/listings/cardoness-castle-walk/) is a 15th-century tower ruin on the banks of the Water of Fleet, less than a mile from the town centre by road. Historic Environment Scotland manages the site; dogs on leads are welcome in the grounds. The drive from Gatehouse takes under five minutes, and the views over the Fleet estuary from the castle are worth the short walk up from the car park.

### Sandgreen Beach and Mossyard Beach

Two beaches sit within easy reach of Gatehouse, both excellent for dogs. [Sandgreen Beach](/listings/sandgreen-beach/) is a sheltered sandy bay popular with families, with good space for dogs to run when the tide is out. [Mossyard Beach](/listings/mossyard-beach/) is quieter and more exposed — a fine stretch of Solway coast with big views and, outside of peak season, a real sense of solitude. Both are a short drive south of the town.

## Dog-Friendly Accommodation in Gatehouse of Fleet

[The Cally Hotel](/listings/cally-hotel-gatehouse-of-fleet/) is a Georgian country house hotel set in 150 acres of grounds on the edge of Gatehouse — an outstanding base for a longer break, with extensive private grounds for exercising dogs and a formal but genuinely welcoming atmosphere. For a more relaxed pitch, [Drumcroy Caravan Park](/listings/drumcroy-caravan-park-gatehouse/) on the edge of the town accepts dogs and is well-placed for exploring the surrounding area.

## Practical Notes for Visiting Gatehouse of Fleet with Your Dog

**Getting there:** Gatehouse of Fleet is on the B796 off the A75, around 30 miles west of Dumfries and 15 miles east of Newton Stewart. Well signposted from both directions.

**Parking:** Free parking is available in the town centre car park off High Street. It fills on summer weekends; arriving before 11am avoids the busiest period.

**Lead rules:** Keep dogs on leads on the High Street and in the town centre. Cardoness Castle requires leads throughout. The beaches are generally relaxed about off-lead dogs, though use your judgement at busy times.

**Best time to visit:** Late spring through early autumn is the sweet spot — Cream o' Galloway is open, the beaches are at their best, and the Galloway hills have long daylight for walking. The town is quieter and equally pleasant in autumn and early winter.

Gatehouse of Fleet rewards return visits. Once you have the layout, you can build a different kind of day each time — castle one morning, beach the next, a different pub each evening. It is a town that earns its place on any list of the best dog-friendly destinations in Scotland.
